Spot a phishing email in 60 seconds

For: Staff with no security training. Time: 60 seconds.

1. Check the sender, not the name

The display name can say GTBank Support, but the real address can be support@gttbank-alerts.com. Tap the name to see the real address before you tap any link.

2. Check for urgent pressure

Your account will be closed today, verify now, urgent action required. Real banks and real companies rarely threaten you in one email.

3. Hover before you click

On a laptop, hover over the link. On a phone, long press it. If the link does not match the company website, do not open it.

4. Check for a strange attachment

Invoice.zip, payment.pdf.exe, and urgent document files are common traps. Do not open them from an unexpected sender.

Your 60 second checklist

  • Real sender address?
  • Urgent threat in the message?
  • Link matches the real website?
  • Unexpected attachment?

If you answer yes to pressure, or no to the sender and link checks, report it and delete it. Do not reply.
